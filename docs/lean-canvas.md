| Block | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| **Problem** | People paid through gigs or informal work cannot rely on a fixed payday. Essential costs such as rent, transport, food and data still arrive on schedule. A balance alone does not show when cash may run short. |
| **Customer segments** | South African gig workers, freelancers, informal traders and households that combine several irregular income sources. Initial segment to test: workers with recurring monthly bills and at least two income sources. |
| **Unique value proposition** | See how recorded earnings, known upcoming costs and an explicit cash assumption affect a 30-day plan. Inspect the reason for a conditional gap or the steps needed before a set-aside estimate is available. |
| **Solution** | Quick income and expense logging; CSV history import; a 30-day planning estimate; a conditional gap without a predicted date; an inspectable set-aside allocation; explicit history and cash assumptions. |
| **Channels** | Direct pilot with worker communities; referrals through community organisations and financial-literacy programmes; potential partnerships with gig platforms after evidence and consent flows are established. |
| **Revenue streams** | Hypothesis: free core cash-flow planning for individuals; later, an opt-in employer/platform or financial-education partnership tier. No pricing or partner demand has been validated. Do not sell personal transaction data. |
| **Cost structure** | Hosting and database, customer support, security and privacy work, product development, and pilot operations. Early costs depend on chosen hosting plans and usage. |
| **Key metrics** | Proposed measures: onboarding completion; users recording income and bills weekly; comprehension of cash/history assumptions; feasibility of acting on a conditional set-aside pace; recorded buffer contributions; 4-week retention; accessibility and low-data task completion. Any later accuracy/calibration claim needs a defined evaluation set and method; none has been measured. |
| **Unfair advantage** | A focused workflow designed for irregular South African cash flow, local currency and language needs, with an explainable estimate. This is a hypothesis until user research and sustained usage demonstrate an advantage. |

## Riskiest assumptions to test

1. People will enter cash-flow data if the benefit arrives before the next bill.
2. A weekly set-aside action is feasible even when income is very small or unpredictable.
3. A transparent rule-based estimate is trusted more than a black-box number.
4. Community partners can help reach users without requiring sensitive bank data.

## First pilot

Use synthetic data during public judging. After the release/privacy checks,
propose a four-week usability pilot with 10–20 consenting participants through
one community partner. Use real data only with explicit informed consent and a
documented deletion path. Compare weekly logging, action completion and
self-reported confidence against each participant's baseline. Publish sample
size, dates and limitations with any result. This pilot is proposed, not run.
