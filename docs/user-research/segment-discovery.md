# Boloka first-user discovery guide

Preparation for issue #75. This guide is a research instrument, not evidence
that interviews have taken place or that a market need has been validated.

## Research question

For one candidate group of people with irregular income, what is the hardest
moment in planning known household costs against payouts that arrive at
different times or in different amounts? What do they do today, and what would
make them trust or reject a planning aid?

Start by recruiting people who identify with gig or freelance work, receive
income from more than one source, and have at least one recurring household
cost. These are screening hypotheses. The team should choose and document the
initial segment after hearing participants' experiences.

## Before speaking with anyone

- Explain who is conducting the research, the purpose, what notes will be
  recorded, who can see them, and how long they will be kept.
- Ask permission before taking notes or recording. Make recording optional.
- Do not request bank logins, account numbers, statements, transaction
  screenshots, exact balances, or identifying financial details.
- Let the participant skip any question or stop at any time.
- Keep contact details separate from anonymized notes and delete them when no
  longer needed for arranging the session.
- Obtain the team's privacy-owner approval for the process before recruitment.

## Interview prompts

Ask about past behavior rather than asking whether a proposed app sounds good.

1. Tell me about the last time income arrived later or in a different amount
   than you expected. What happened next?
2. How do you decide which costs to pay first when money is uneven?
3. Which costs are predictable, and which change from month to month?
4. What tools, notes, people, or routines do you use today?
5. What is frustrating or risky about that approach?
6. What information do you trust when planning? What information would you
   rather not enter into an app?
7. When would you want help: when a payout arrives, before a bill, or at a
   regular weekly check-in? Why?
8. If you saw a 30-day estimate based on your recorded history and listed
   costs, what would you need to understand before acting on it?
9. What would make recording information feel worthwhile, and what would make
   you stop using the tool?
10. What phone, connectivity, language, or accessibility needs should the team
    account for?

## Notes template

For each session record only:

- Participant alias, broad segment and session date.
- Consent to notes (yes/no) and recording (yes/no); do not record if consent
  is absent.
- Recent situation described, current workaround, pain point and impact in
  the participant's own words where safe to quote.
- What information they would enter, what they would not enter, and why.
- Preferred moment and frequency for using a planning tool.
- Accessibility, language and connectivity needs.
- Researcher observations, alternative explanations and unresolved questions.

Do not include account identifiers, exact transaction details or other
unnecessary personal data in the research record.

## Synthesis and acceptance for #75

After the sessions, summarize repeated patterns and meaningful disagreements.
Record participant count, recruitment approach, dates, limitations and the
evidence behind the selected segment and value proposition. A small discovery
sample can guide product decisions; it cannot establish market size, adoption,
retention or financial impact.

Proposed value-proposition format:

> For [specific people] who struggle with [observed planning moment], Boloka
> helps them [observable task] using [specific product mechanism], while being
> clear about [important limitation].

Replace every bracket with evidence from the sessions before presenting this
as a validated positioning statement.
